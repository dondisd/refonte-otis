New York · Crystal Palace · May 21, 1854
// 01 · the rope

Every elevator asks you to trust a rope.

This is an elevator shaft, drawn the way a patent draws it: the car, its two guide rails, the sheave, and the single rope holding all of it up. Descend, and watch what happens to the rope.

// 02 · the cut

He ordered the rope cut.

Not a model. Not a diagram. His own platform, his own weight, a crowd underneath and an axe above.

// 03 · a few inches

It fell four inches and stopped.

The safety gear has bitten into both guide rails. Keep going: the car will not drop another millimetre, however hard you push. That is the entire product.

// 04 · the vertical city

The same catch, 2.5 million times.

Otis holds the largest installed base on earth, and it moves 2.5 billion people a day.

// 05 · the 1853 test bench

Cut the rope yourself.

Load the car, set its rated speed, trip the governor. The numbers are the real ones.

The drop does not change with load. Move the load from 200 to 2000 kg and the millimetres hold. What changes is the force the safety gear has to develop against the rail.
